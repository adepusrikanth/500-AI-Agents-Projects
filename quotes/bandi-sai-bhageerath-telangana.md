# Quote on the Bandi Sai Bhageerath POCSO Case, Telangana (May 2026)

> **"When the son of a Union Minister evades arrest for days under a POCSO case while ordinary citizens are jailed overnight for far lesser offences, the law does not fail -- it simply reveals whose side it was always on. Power does not bend to justice; it bends justice to itself. In Telangana today, a 17-year-old girl waits for the law to find its courage, while the accused hides behind anticipatory bail, counter-complaints, and the longest arm any criminal can have -- political privilege. The real crime is not just what happened at that farmhouse; it is a system where the powerful write missing-person posters for accountability itself."**

---

## Context

In May 2026, **Bandi Sai Bhageerath** (age 23), son of Union Minister of State for Home Affairs **Bandi Sanjay Kumar**, was booked under the **POCSO Act** (Protection of Children from Sexual Offences) for allegedly sexually assaulting a 17-year-old girl at a farmhouse. The charges were later upgraded to **aggravated penetrative sexual assault** under Section 5(i) read with Section 6 of the POCSO Act, carrying up to **20 years of imprisonment**.

### What happened:

- **Despite the severity of the charges**, Telangana police failed to arrest Bhageerath even five days after the FIR was filed.
- He **skipped police summons**, prompting a multi-city manhunt across Karimnagar, Hyderabad, and Delhi.
- He filed for **anticipatory bail** in the Telangana High Court and lodged a **counter-complaint** alleging extortion against the victim's family.
- **"Missing" posters** of Bhageerath appeared in public areas, put up by citizens and women's groups demanding justice.
- Women's groups **staged protests** outside the Petbasheerabad police station.

### The deeper issue:

This case lays bare a painful truth about the Indian justice system: **power determines the speed of the law**. When a common person is accused under POCSO, arrest is swift and non-negotiable. When the accused is the son of a sitting Union Minister, the system offers anticipatory bail hearings, delays, and the luxury of evasion.

The victim -- a minor girl who reportedly attempted suicide after the relationship ended and private photographs were allegedly circulated -- represents the voiceless millions who discover that the law protects the powerful first and the powerless only when it is convenient.
